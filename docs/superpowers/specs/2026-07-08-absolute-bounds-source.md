## Methodology

These are ABSOLUTE PLAUSIBILITY BOUNDS for the R13 OCR-safety rule across all 89 analytes, expressed in the SI unit keyed to each row. WHAT THEY ARE: hard min/max "absurd-value" limits, in the tradition of clinical-laboratory autoverification (Krasowski/Univ. of Iowa core-lab, PMC4023033; Vis 2016 hematology autoverification), each placed just OUTSIDE the most extreme value ever documented in a living human plus margin, and cross-checked against analytical measuring/reportable ranges (including on-board dilution ceilings). Their sole job is to catch magnitude-absurd OCR misreads - decimal shifts, inserted/dropped digits, wrong-row values (e.g. K 4.0->40, TSH 4.0->4000, CK 150->1500000). WHAT THEY ARE NOT: they are NOT reference intervals, NOT panic/critical thresholds, and NOT strict biological-impossibility limits. They are deliberately far wider than any reference range so that every real survivable or critical result falls INSIDE and is never suppressed. Design principle (conservative-wide / widen-first): when documented extremes and reportable caps disagreed, the more extreme value plus margin was taken; when in doubt the bound was widened. A direct consequence is that for a handful of analytes whose real survivable range spans orders of magnitude (triglycerides to >113 mmol/L in familial chylomicronemia, fasting insulin to ~16000-18000 mIU/L in overdose survivors, ferritin to 256000 ug/L in HLH/AOSD, troponin/CK to >1e6, differential percentages bounded only 0-100), a 10x decimal shift will NOT always trip the bound - this is intentional and explicitly safer than risking suppression of a genuine pancreatitis-risk, insulin-overdose, or massive-necrosis value. Representative anchors: potassium widened from the naive 1-10 to 1.0-15 because 14.0 mmol/L is a documented survivor; creatinine 7000 umol/L clears the 64.6 mg/dL record; glucose 160 mmol/L clears the ~148 Guinness record; ESR 200 mm/h is a genuine Westergren physical cap; ALP 20000 U/L is driven by benign transient hyperphosphatasemia of infancy (14589 U/L), not adult disease. As lead I applied three additional cross-group widenings beyond the group submissions, all noted in-row as LEAD-WIDENED: (1) chloride ceiling 150->170 mmol/L so it can track the widened sodium ceiling (230) in severe hypernatremic dehydration; (2) transferrin_saturation ceiling 150->200% because in iron poisoning serum iron genuinely exceeds TIBC, producing real supraphysiologic saturation, while 45->450 still trips; (3) total_t4 floor 3->1 nmol/L because severe myxedema TT4 can be near-undetectable, aligning it with the near-zero floors of the free thyroid hormones. Four analytes (urine_protein, urine_glucose, urine_ketones, urine_specific_gravity) are qualitative dipstick / narrow-ratio fields with no meaningful continuous numeric bound and are nulled and listed separately; urine_ph, by contrast, retains a tight 4.0-9.0 bound from hard renal-physiology limits and is an effective OCR check. IMPORTANT LIMITATION carried from every group: R13 is a magnitude gate only and does NOT catch unit/scale confusion, which can make a mislabeled value read plausibly within bounds. Flagged unit traps requiring a SEPARATE unit-consistency check: urea vs urea-nitrogen/BUN (x2.14), uric acid umol/L vs mmol/L (1000x), ACR mg/mmol vs mg/g (x8.84), calcium mg/dL vs mEq/L, magnesium mg/dL vs mmol/L, D-dimer FEU vs DDU (~2x) and ng/mL vs mg/L (1000x), serum iron/TIBC umol/L vs ug/dL (~5.6x), C-peptide ng/mL vs nmol/L (x0.331), insulin mIU/L vs pmol/L (/~6), Lp(a) mg/dL vs nmol/L (non-interconvertible), B12 pmol/L vs pg/mL (~0.738x), folate/vitamin-D nmol/L vs ng/mL (~2.27x / 2.5x), and CK-MB activity (U/L) vs mass (ng/mL). Bounds are pooled across ages and pregnancy so neonatal/pediatric physiology (higher phosphate/calcium/ALP/bilirubin, lower creatinine) and pregnancy (lower creatinine/urea, higher eGFR/ALP/bile-acids) all fall inside. Values are assay- and method-specific; where a lab prints its own technical/reportable limits those take precedence. To embed in data/reference-labs.ts: these are living-patient OCR-plausibility limits, not clinical ranges, and passing a bound does NOT validate a value.

## Caveats (safety-critical)

R13 MUST SUPPRESS, NEVER SILENTLY DROP: when a value falls outside its plausibility bound, the pipeline must withhold/flag interpretation and surface the raw extracted value with a "could not verify - please re-check the original report" state, never quietly discard or auto-correct it. R13 MUST NEVER SUPPRESS A REAL CRITICAL VALUE: the bounds are set wide precisely so that documented survivable/critical extremes (K 14, glucose 148, TSH ~1100, CK >1.6M, undetectable fibrinogen in DIC, near-zero HDL in Tangier, etc.) pass through to interpretation - if you are ever tempted to tighten a bound toward a reference or panic range, do NOT, because that would convert a life-threatening true result into a suppressed one. PREGNANCY/PEDIATRIC WIDENING: bounds are pooled across ages and gestation and already accommodate neonatal/child physiology (physiologically higher phosphate, calcium, ALP up to thousands of U/L from transient hyperphosphatasemia of infancy, neonatal hyperbilirubinemia to kernicterus range, higher WBC/lymphocytes/RBC/MCV, lower creatinine) and pregnancy (lower creatinine/urea/albumin, higher eGFR, higher ALP and bile acids from placental isoenzyme/ICP) - never re-narrow for an "adult" assumption. SPECIMEN/PRE-ANALYTIC EFFECTS produce REAL measured values that correctly stay inside bounds and must NOT be treated as errors to suppress: in-vitro hemolysis raising AST/LDH/K, EDTA storage swelling MPV or clumping lowering platelets, cold-agglutinin/lipemia inflating MCV/MCH/MCHC, pseudohyperkalemia from thrombocytosis/leukocytosis, halide interference on chloride, rasburicase degrading urate in vitro, heterophile/paraprotein interference on TT3/thyroid antibodies, delayed serum separation raising homocysteine. UNIT/SCALE CONFUSION IS THE MAIN RESIDUAL RISK R13 CANNOT CATCH: a mislabeled-unit value can read plausibly within bounds (urea vs BUN x2.14, uric acid/B12/folate/vitamin-D/iron SI-vs-conventional factors, D-dimer FEU vs DDU, Lp(a) mg/dL vs nmol/L, insulin mIU/L vs pmol/L, C-peptide ng/mL vs nmol/L, CK-MB activity vs mass, HCT L/L vs %) - these require a separate unit-consistency check upstream of R13, not tighter magnitude bounds. QUALITATIVE FIELDS (urine protein/glucose/ketones and specific gravity) have no numeric bound; "negative" is normal and must be encoded via the negative-normal categorical convention, not as a 0-valued range, and R13 provides no protection for them. Finally, a value passing its bound is NOT thereby validated as correct - R13 is a numeric-integrity gate only and never a substitute for clinical interpretation.
